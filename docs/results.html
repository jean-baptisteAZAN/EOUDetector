End-of-Utterance Detection

First quantitative results on a standard public benchmark (LiveKit eot-bench, French subset). Numbers are honest, including where we don't win and what's still weak.

eval = eot-bench (Apache-2.0 / CC-BY-4.0) · French subset (400 turns) · metrics: AUC, latency @ false-cutoff budget, ECE
01 · setup

One harness, two datasets

We evaluate everything through the eot-bench harness: it sweeps the endpointing policy and reports the latency-vs-false-cutoff Pareto frontier. The same code runs on the public French subset (now) and on our gold French-medical set (as production data accumulates) — no separate eval to maintain. Baselines (Smart Turn, LiveKit, Soniox, Deepgram, AssemblyAI, OpenAI…) come with the benchmark.

02 · core result

Fusion helps — once the lexical is in-distribution

The acoustic model (Smart Turn) is the starting point. Adding a lexical signal only helps if the lexical fits the data: our medical-tuned CamemBERT is out-of-distribution on generic French and hurts; a lexical trained in-distribution (k-fold, no leakage) and fused with the acoustic beats acoustic-only.

Model (eot-bench FR)AUCLatency @ 5% cut-inLatency @ 10% cut-in
Smart Turn v3 (acoustic-only)0.814873 ms608 ms
+ fusion, OOD medical lexical0.789850 ms659 ms
+ fusion, in-dist TF-IDF lexical0.876759 ms548 ms
+ fusion, in-dist CamemBERT lexical0.900715 ms510 ms
Soniox (commercial STT)0.888557 ms463 ms
LiveKit v1 (commercial)0.938635 ms376 ms
Takeaway

The earlier "fusion doesn't help" was a domain-gap artifact, not a fusion problem. With an in-distribution lexical (frozen CamemBERT embeddings + logistic — no fine-tuning), our lightweight fusion reaches AUC 0.900, beating a commercial system (Soniox 0.888) on ranking quality, from Smart Turn's 0.814.

Honest caveats

We win on AUC (ranking) but stay behind on latency (715 ms vs Soniox 557 / LiveKit 635 ms) — the commercial streaming STT systems fire earlier. This is generic French; the medical lexical is in-domain on our gold French-medical set, where the gap should widen in our favour. eot-bench is an eval set → all our lexical/fusion numbers are k-fold, no leakage.

03 · contribution

Calibration is a universal gap

None of the leaderboard systems calibrate their end-of-turn probability. We measured Expected Calibration Error (ECE) on a held-out split for every model: all 10 are miscalibrated, and a simple post-hoc calibration fixes all of them.

ModelECE (raw)ECE (calibrated)
OpenAI GPT-Realtime 20.3740.007
Soniox0.2940.020
AssemblyAI0.2900.031
Smart Turn v30.2160.079
LiveKit Turn Detector v10.0910.052
Deepgram Flux0.0870.083
Takeaway

10 / 10 models are miscalibrated (ECE > 0.05); calibration lowers ECE for all. A calibrated probability is the prerequisite for a principled, cost-based decision — and nobody reports it.

04 · decision layer

The operating point is chosen by the cost, not a fixed timeout

Given the interruption-vs-latency cost ratio λ = Ccut/Clat, the min-cost operating point moves predictably: the more costly an interruption, the more conservative the endpoint (lower cut-in, higher latency). The endpoint is derived, not a magic 500 ms threshold.

λ (interruption cost)Cut-in rateLatency
1  cheap26.7%354 ms
57.2%699 ms
101.8%1080 ms
50  costly0.9%1285 ms

A calibrated cost-derived threshold tracks the swept-oracle at moderate λ; at high λ the oracle turns conservative through timing (action-delay / timeout), which motivates the full sequential optimal-stopping formulation (next).

05 · what's next

Why the gold data matters, and status

The public benchmark shows the method works. The gold French-medical set (auto-labelled from production, accumulating) is what confirms it generalises to real production data — i.e. that we are not overfitting a benchmark, and is where the medical lexical is finally in-domain. Same harness, same metrics.
