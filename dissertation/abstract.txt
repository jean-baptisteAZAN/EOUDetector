Towards Semantically-Aware End-of-Utterance Detection for Conversational AI Systems
Jean-Baptiste Azan - MSc Artificial Intelligence - University of Kent

ABSTRACT

A conversational voice agent has to decide, in real time, when a caller has finished speaking. On a French medical telephone line that decision is delicate: ending a turn too early cuts a patient off part-way through a spelled name or a dictated number, while ending it too late makes the exchange feel slow. Systems that rely on silence alone hear sound but not meaning, and cut off utterances that are grammatically incomplete.

This dissertation asks whether adding a lexical, meaning-aware signal improves that decision, and how that probability should become an endpoint. Two off-the-shelf models, an acoustic end-of-turn detector and a French language model reading the partial transcript, are fused into one probability; that probability is calibrated; and the operating point is chosen, in the offline evaluation, from an explicit interruption-versus-latency cost rather than from a timer. No base model is fine-tuned in the pipeline that produces this result, and no large language model sits in the decision loop.

On a public French benchmark the fusion raises the per-span AUC from 0.814 to 0.897, a gain of 0.083 (95 % confidence interval [0.063, 0.104], p < 1/3000, bootstrap resampled by call). Across the leaderboard, calibration proves a field-wide blind spot: every external detector evaluated is miscalibrated and none reports it. Validation on 636 production medical calls is negative, the acoustic branch saturating on 8 kHz telephony and weak supervision capping the lexical signal, marking where the approach does not transfer. The contribution is a calibration-first, cost-aware method for composing existing models, with its limits measured.
